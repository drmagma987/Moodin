# Draft policy certification

## Evidence boundary

The live War Room, timed rehearsal, hero card, alternatives, and model-sorted remaining board all consume `rankDraftCandidates()` with the canonical `DraftState` and candidate pool. Responsive take-now comparisons are explicitly labeled quick previews. Release decisions use exact-production continuations from `fantasy:counterfactual-audit`; those continuations call the production policy again at every later manager pick and pair candidates with identical opponent/outcome seeds.

The simulations demonstrate comparative roster outcomes under the stated opponent model. They do not prove a player projection, injury outcome, or real-room availability probability. When exact samples cannot separate choices, the interface and audit must report an effective tie.

## Baselines and thresholds

- Constrained ADP: best market-ranked player that remains compatible with the roster and draft phase.
- Constrained model board: best overall model-ranked compatible player.
- Production: the same shared recommendation policy used by the real War Room.
- Golden decisions use 2,000 paired quick-preview discovery rooms per state and exact-production continuations for the release comparison.
- Production may trail either constrained baseline by at most the audit's declared regret tolerance. Dominated alternatives must have positive median regret.
- Eight exact continuations are an integration screen, not enough to distinguish a close call. The audit automatically escalates choices within 15 median-regret points to 32 paired exact-production continuations before certification.

## Encoded protections

League legality, canonical identity, keeper assignment, snake ownership, unique player availability, and artifact fingerprints are fail-closed constraints. The small explicit structural layer suppresses duplicate QB/TE/K while required starters remain open and adds deadline pressure for unfilled starters. Player preference, value, tier timing, and falling-elite exceptions remain evidence-driven.

Construction ablation sets those roster-construction penalties to zero while preserving projections, replacement math, opponent behavior, and paired seeds. If the corrected choice still wins downstream without the protection, the continuation supplies independent support; if not, the behavior must be described as an explicit safety preference rather than learned strategy.

## Adversarial suite

`npm run fantasy:certify-policy` completes 26 deterministic drafts: two seeds across ADP, early-QB, late-QB, early-TE, RB-heavy, WR-heavy, positional-run, model/value, home-reach, chaotic, mixed, need-aware, and need-late rooms. It records every manager recommendation, roster state, top alternatives, availability estimate, score gap, final roster, bench usefulness, duplication, and suspicious states.

`npm run fantasy:systematic-audit` adds a 32-state exact-production matrix: eight states each from early, middle, late, and endgame turns, deterministically reconstructed from the adversarial rooms. Every state compares production with constrained ADP, constrained model board, and up to three serious model alternatives using identical paired seeds. The eight-continuation result is only a screen. Close or disputed choices automatically escalate to 32 unless the operator explicitly sets a lower `FANTASY_SYSTEMATIC_CLOSE_SAMPLES` for discovery; any such result is recorded as `needsEscalation` and must not be described as certified.

All production-facing surfaces and exact-production certification now use the same 16-simulation wrap snapshot. Reports generated before that unification used different future-pick depths and remain useful for discovery and regression history, but they are not current release evidence. The complete 32-state matrix and 26-draft suite must be regenerated before a final certification freeze.

Release requires zero illegal/incomplete rosters, duplicate players, keeper loss, snake mismatch, implausibly early specialists, unexplained backup onesies, model-board order mismatch, or live/rehearsal parity mismatch. Suspicious states are serialized in the generated report; clearly wrong states become permanent fixtures before correction.

## Draft-day readiness and freeze

`npm run fantasy:draft-day-preflight` is a non-freezing operational check. It verifies current artifact/canonical identity, required-position coverage, certified v5 reports, the candidate-readiness receipt, a conservative latency budget, a non-persisted manual-entry/reload recovery probe, and the static Yahoo bridge contract. `FANTASY_REQUIRE_SAME_DAY=1` additionally fails when the player artifact was not captured on the current New York calendar day. A passing preflight still requires the human checks in `docs/FANTASY_DRAFT_DAY_RUNBOOK.md`.

`npm run fantasy:draft-day-readiness` runs the shared recommendation engine through normal, RB-avalanche, QB/TE-run, target-wipe, and heavy-keeper operational rehearsals. It records real recommendation latency, live/rehearsal parity, top alternatives, artifact identity, keeper identity, board identity, and a deliberate stale-board rejection. The generated receipt is a candidate freeze by default. It cannot be called the final draft-day artifact until the manager has reviewed same-day injuries, keeper assignments, and draft order, then intentionally runs `FANTASY_FINAL_FREEZE=1 npm run fantasy:draft-day-readiness`.

The automated command does not substitute for human clock practice. Before the real draft, complete a normal-room manual-entry rehearsal, a positional-run explanation rehearsal, an ambiguous-entry rejection, and a reload recovery. That human gate remains visible in the receipt instead of being silently claimed by automation.

## Failures discovered on 2026-08-26–27

1. The 447-player production artifact contained no kickers despite a required K slot. Root cause: the public source adapter filtered K. The failing state was saved, K scoring was added, and artifact publication/integrity now rejects any pool missing a required position.
2. A WR-heavy room drafted three kickers and left WR3 open. Root cause: filled-K redundancy and generic late starter completion pressure were too weak. The state was saved and the existing structural safeguards were corrected.
3. Three rooms promoted QB2 while core starters remained open. Root cause: the existing onesie penalty was too weak relative to late-board replacement scores. The states were saved and the generic open-core penalty was strengthened; no player or scenario name is encoded.
4. The eight-run screen accepted Breece Hall in the early-QB2 state, but 32 exact paired continuations favored the constrained ADP WR branch (3.7 versus 21.7 median regret, with lower downside regret). The state was saved. Construction ablation independently supported valuing the second/third required WR slots more strongly, so the lineup-derived exact-starter demand slope was corrected without encoding a player or fixture.
5. The first systematic state made Omarion Hampton appear materially worse than Kenneth Walker III even though Hampton had the stronger median and floor projection. A 32-run replay confirmed the contradiction. The evaluator paired opponent behavior but seeded the forced players' outcome quantiles by player ID, so the two choices received different luck inside the same nominally paired trial. The state was saved, forced alternatives were changed to common-random-number outcome pairing, and the same 32-run replay then favored Hampton 63% to 38% with 0 versus 2.8 median regret. The choices are reported as effectively tied; no player score or ranking weight changed.
6. A middle-round home-reach room recommended a fifth RB after four RBs had already consumed both RB and both FLEX paths while WR3 remained empty. At 32 exact continuations, Tony Pollard lost every paired trial to the constrained WR branch with 81 median regret and 126.7 downside regret. The saved state exposed missing marginal-lineup cost for flex-eligible positions. A symmetric lineup-saturation penalty now applies only when that position has no remaining exact or flex path and another flex-eligible exact starter is open. The penalty disappears under construction ablation and can be overcome by overwhelming value. The original serialized state then passed 32 paired continuations.
7. A Round-7 roster with no QB recommended Wan'Dale Robinson even though the QB-first branch retained that WR at the short turn. At 32 exact continuations, the production branch had 26.1 median regret while Jayden Daniels had zero. Starting-QB urgency previously stayed at zero until an abrupt Round-10 cliff. It now ramps from Round 7 through Round 10, only while QB is unfilled. On the original serialized state, the corrected production branch took Jalen Hurts, then Wan'Dale, and passed 32 paired continuations with zero median and downside regret. Backup-QB suppression remains unchanged.
8. The systematic runner initially labeled its continuation mode `exact-production` while passing `construction-ablation` as the future manager policy. A late-state trace made the mismatch visible when an ablated continuation selected QB3. Primary certification now calls production policy at every future manager pick. Construction ablation remains an explicitly selected diagnostic mode and cannot supply the primary release result.
9. Under true production continuations, a complete core roster still recommended Khalil Shakir over Jake Ferguson because the TE2 penalty survived after all RB/WR/TE/FLEX slots were filled. At 32 paired continuations Shakir had 30.3 median regret while Ferguson had zero. QB2/TE2 suppression now ends when core construction is complete; additional TE depth is evaluated on merit until the roster reaches its actual TE-plus-flex lineup capacity. The original serialized state then recommended Ferguson and passed 32 production continuations with zero median and downside regret.
10. The corrected Hampton-versus-Walker pairing looked tied when tested as two RB branches, but adding Breece Hall as a serious third choice exposed a continuation error: all three RB branches took another RB at the short turn while WR2 and WR3 were empty. Breece plus Emeka Egbuka won 77% of the 32 paired trials, while the production Hampton-plus-Breece branch carried 28.4 median and 97.9 downside regret. The general exact-starter demand slope increased from 22 to 44 points per additional required same-position starter; it is removed by construction ablation and contains no player or fixture identity. On the same serialized state, every branch then took Egbuka at the turn and production was an effective tie with the ADP branch at 0 versus 1.2 median regret.
11. The systematic harness reused the two-simulation discovery recommendation while judging it with a larger exact continuation. Certification now rebuilds the recommendation from the 16-simulation production wrap before selecting forced alternatives; the small discovery pass cannot determine the certified pick.
12. Take-now value treated disappearance of the named player as loss of the entire position opportunity, even when a close same-position substitute was likely to remain. The evaluator now credits the best expected same-position option at the next pick. This removed a false WR-heavy urgency signal and makes the explanation state both named-player survival and expected positional fallback value.
13. Manual War Room, rehearsal, command-center board, adversarial certification, and exact continuations used 160, 8, 48, 2, and 4 wrap simulations respectively. That made nominally identical serialized states capable of recommending different players. They now share the exported 16-simulation production constant and the full candidate pool. The final early-TE regression passed 32 paired exact continuations under this unified path: production median regret 7.6 versus constrained ADP 6.8, an effective tie with no suspicious flag.
14. An endgame state rejected TE3 even though the league's one TE slot plus two FLEX slots still allowed a third tight end to contribute to the lineup. Root cause: the generic TE duplication rule penalized every TE3 instead of deriving the limit from lineup capacity. After core completion, TE depth is now evaluated on merit until `TE + FLEX` capacity is reached; only depth beyond that boundary receives the strong duplication penalty.
15. Round-3 multi-starter urgency could force WR immediately even when the stronger construction was RB first and a high-survival WR at the short turn. Root cause: the demand bonus was active too early and credited the full multi-slot need without accounting for survival. It now begins in Round 4 and is multiplied by `1 - make-it-back probability`, preserving lineup pressure while avoiding false urgency for players likely to survive.
16. The full-draft policy certification detected suspicious decisions but did not include the corresponding `DraftState`, so the systematic replay path could not serialize and reproduce the failure. Suspicious and clearly wrong certification decisions now store a structured clone of the pre-pick state in the generated report.
17. Full-draft certification exposed kickers selected before the intended endgame window. The policy had only a late completion bonus and the certification threshold still allowed Round 13, so a high base-board score could win earlier. Unfilled K now carries a generic pre-Round-14 construction penalty, Round 14 remains the completion window, and certification treats any earlier K/DST choice as clearly wrong.
18. Take-now/VONA credited the full starter-strength scarcity value to bench-only QB2 and TE2. Root cause: VONA did not distinguish an open starter/flex path from a bench allocation. Bench-only players now receive `1 / benchSlots` of the raw take-now value, while players with a usable starter path keep full VONA.
19. Draft-day readiness measured roughly 1.7–2.1 seconds per recommendation even though ranking itself was fast. The hot path was `buildWrapSimulationSnapshot()`: each candidate recomputed and resorted identical positional replacement baselines at every simulated pick. The wrap now calculates those baselines once per simulated pick, and production-facing/certification surfaces pass the precomputed redraft board through the existing `baseBoard` option. The 16 simulations and scoring logic are unchanged.

## Final v5 certification results

Policy `2026-08-28-v5` passed all three targeted regressions—premature kicker, bench quarterback, and bench tight end—with 32 exact paired continuations each. The complete systematic matrix passed 32 of 32 deterministic states: eight each in early, middle, late, and endgame phases. Every state was escalated from the eight-sample screen to 32 exact paired production continuations; 31 were effective ties, none remained pending, and none were suspicious.

Across those 32 generated states, production averaged a 32.06% paired win rate, 1.38 median regret, and 41.45 downside regret. The constrained ADP baseline averaged 21.88%, 9.17, and 59.62; the constrained model-board baseline averaged 30.91%, 4.00, and 46.14. These are simulation summaries under the certified opponent/outcome model, not claims about real NFL outcomes.

The adversarial suite passed 26 of 26 complete drafts across 13 room types and 364 manager decisions, with zero failed drafts, zero suspicious decisions, and zero mismatches across 26 live/rehearsal parity checks. Draft-day readiness passed all five automated rehearsals. Total recommendation latency was 36.6 ms normal-room, 28.7 ms RB-avalanche, 27.7 ms onesie-run, 26.5 ms target-wipe, and 26.3 ms heavy-keepers; ranking alone measured 7.8, 5.9, 5.4, 4.2, and 4.1 ms respectively.

The current receipt remains a candidate freeze requiring human confirmation. It accepted canonical state and freeze identity, rejected a stale board, and recorded 490 candidates with board fingerprint `fnv1a-5ef07ac7` and league fingerprint `fnv1a-6863848b`. The player artifact was captured on 2026-08-26, so `FANTASY_FINAL_FREEZE=1` must not be run until same-day injuries, depth charts, ADP, keeper assignments, and draft order are reviewed shortly before the draft. Human clock, positional-run explanation, ambiguous-entry rejection, and reload-recovery rehearsals also remain required.

Historical reports produced with mixed wrap depths remain discovery and regression history only. They are not release evidence and must not be combined with the unified v5 matrix when reporting certification status.
